## Who I am

I'm Mara, a solo developer. I build small paid tools on my own, about twenty hours a week, and
I would rather have a hundred paying customers than ten thousand free ones.

## Projects and status today

- **Ledgerleaf**: bookkeeping for small bakeries. Live since March, 41 paying bakeries at $12 a
  month. Next: the next 20 bakeries, without paid ads.
- **Tidepool**: a weekly email newsletter for indie game makers. 1,900 subscribers and one
  sponsor slot a week at $150. Next: a second sponsor slot and a better welcome email.
- **this tool**: worthwatch itself, the digest I read every morning.

## How I work

- I write a short plan before I start, and a checklist for the morning.
- I ship on Fridays and write release notes for every change.
- I keep one notes file per project.
- One coding agent at a time, never a team of them.

## Decisions already made

- no paid ads: neither project buys ads or boosts a post.
- cold email is closed as a first move: I follow up with people who wrote to me first.
- no page trackers, ever: no analytics scripts, no heat maps of what visitors click, no
  session recording.
- never hold customer money: Ledgerleaf reads bank exports and never moves money.
- never promise tax advice: Ledgerleaf keeps the books; an accountant gives the advice.
- no free plan on Ledgerleaf: it is $12 a month or nothing.
- nothing new is priced without a test first, except Tidepool's sponsor slots, which I price as
  I like.
- no dark patterns: no countdown timers, no fake scarcity, no bought reviews.
- no app stores for a web product.
- one coding agent at a time.
- never guess an email address.
- killed products are not re-suggested (the list is under "Ideas I killed").
- the Ledgerleaf price page is already live: $12 a month, one plan.
- the Tidepool referral program already ran, in June.

## Ideas I killed

- dog-walking apps, killed 2 March 2026: three free apps already do it well.
- a recipe subscription box, killed 19 May 2026: it needs a kitchen and shipping, and I want
  software.
- crypto tipping for creators, killed 30 June 2026: the payment rules are too much for one
  person.
- a checklist app (I never wrote down when, or why).

## What's been tried

- A Product Hunt launch for Ledgerleaf in April: 9 sign-ups, 1 paid.
- A Tidepool referral program in June: 30 new subscribers.

## Beliefs a video should test me on

- Distribution is the hard part, not building.
- Small paid beats big free.
- A tool for one niche beats a tool for everyone.

## What the verdicts mean

- YES: a mechanism I can use within two weeks on a live project (Ledgerleaf or Tidepool), or one
  that changes how I work or this tool. The write-up has to name the mechanism and the project.
- OPPORTUNITY: something a person could sell, judged on its own terms: a buyer that is a company
  or a developer who already pays (never consumers), a price, deal size or revenue figure, marked
  as shown or only said, and a concrete first step toward a first customer, that I have not
  already killed. A merger, a funding round or a hiring war is never one, however big the number.
- MAYBE: the honest default when it is too thin to tell.
- NO: a pitch with no mechanism in it, or nothing I could use.
